# Required landmarks and adjacencies

A missing **defining** landmark, or a structure floating where it should touch its neighbour, is a blocker. For each subject, check every item before you build. For subjects not listed here, write an equivalent list from real references first.

## Neuro

**Coronal brain slice (level of thalamus and hippocampus)**
- Longitudinal fissure, ending at the **corpus callosum**. The callosum is the white roof of the lateral ventricles; cortex never touches a ventricle.
- Septum pellucidum between the lateral ventricles. The third ventricle is in the midline below.
- **Caudate** in the inferolateral wall of each lateral ventricle, never an island inside it.
- Internal capsule, then putamen and globus pallidus, external capsule, claustrum, extreme capsule, **insula**.
- **Sylvian fissure** shaped like a T or Y, continuing into the circular sulcus around the insula.
- **Thalami** form the walls of the third ventricle and the floor of the lateral ventricle bodies.
- Hypothalamus at the base. It tapers, carries mammillary bumps and has **no cortical rim**. Optic tracts sit on the ventral surface.
- **Hippocampus** forms the floor and medial wall of the temporal horn. Draw it with Ammon's horn (a pyramidal band) and the dentate gyrus. It is continuous with the subiculum and parahippocampal cortex.
- Cingulate gyrus above the callosum.
- Sulci of very different depth: superior frontal, cingulate, superior temporal and collateral are deep. Some branch, spacing is uneven, and gyri are wide enough to carry a white-matter core.
- Cortical ribbon about 1/60 of the slice width.
- **Alzheimer's disease:** widened sulci, thinner cortex, ventricles enlarged *ex vacuo* (they still hug the caudate and hippocampus), and a shrunken hippocampus still lining the horn. The caudate is not flattened; that is Huntington disease.

**Cortical section (grey matter)**
- Pia and meninges on top; layer I is cell-sparse.
- **Pyramidal neurons**: triangular soma, nucleus with nucleolus, apical dendrite toward the pia, tapered basal dendrites. The axon leaves a small hillock at the soma base, has uniform calibre and runs down past the dendrites.
- Interneurons, oligodendrocytes (perinuclear halo), and ramified microglia with fine processes.
- **Capillary profiles**: one flattened endothelial nucleus, optionally a pericyte, and astrocyte end-feet covering at least 270° of the outer wall.
- Penetrating arterioles **branch or leave the frame**.
- **Alzheimer's disease:**
  - Dense-cored plaques: an irregular core about 26–30% of the plaque diameter with short fibrillar spikes and a diffuse corona. Dystrophic neurites are swellings on neurites that enter from outside.
  - Diffuse plaques: ill-defined, with no outline in line style.
  - Neurofibrillary tangles: a flame **inside** the soma, continuing up the apical dendrite and inset from the membrane.
  - Ghost tangles: paler and looser.
  - Neuropil threads: varied short curly fragments.
  - Reactive astrocytes: a hypertrophic soma about 2.5× the nucleus, with thick, uneven processes.
  - Activated microglia contacting plaques: amoeboid, with a kidney-shaped nucleus.
  - CAA lies within the vessel media, not outside it.

## Kidney cortex

- The glomerular tuft fills Bowman's capsule, leaving a thin crescent of Bowman's space.
- Parietal epithelium is flat.
- Podocyte nuclei sit on the outer (urinary) side of the loops.
- Mesangium is central; capillary loops contain RBCs.
- **Vascular pole**: the afferent arteriole (thicker media) and efferent arteriole enter together, with the macula densa (crowded distal-tubule nuclei) beside them.
- Proximal tubules: tall, eosinophilic, brush border, small lumen.
- Distal tubules: lower cells, clear lumen.
- Peritubular capillaries between the tubules.
- **Glomerulonephritis**:
  - crescents of proliferating parietal cells and macrophages, with fibrin;
  - a hypercellular tuft;
  - RBC or protein casts in the tubules;
  - interstitial inflammation and fibrosis.

## Airway

- Pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium with goblet cells and basal cells.
- Thin basement membrane over the lamina propria.
- Smooth-muscle bundle.
- Submucosal glands with mucous (pale) and serous (pink) acini.
- Cartilage plate with chondrocytes in lacunae and a perichondrium.
- **Asthma**:
  - a mucus plug with eosinophils and shed epithelium (Creola bodies);
  - goblet-cell hyperplasia;
  - a thick sub-epithelial basement membrane;
  - smooth-muscle hypertrophy;
  - eosinophils and mast cells;
  - a folded mucosa.

## Joints and synovium

These matter most to this project's user (an RA synovial-tissue researcher).

**Synovium**
- **Healthy**: a lining layer 1–3 cells thick, mixing macrophage-like (MLS) and fibroblast-like (FLS) synoviocytes on a loose sublining with adipocytes, vessels and scattered fibroblasts. There is no basement membrane under the lining.
- **RA**:
  - lining hyperplasia (more than 3 cell layers) and villous projections;
  - neoangiogenesis;
  - **pathotypes** (Pitzalis/Humby):
    - *lympho-myeloid*: B/T-cell aggregates, often with germinal-centre-like structures and plasma cells;
    - *diffuse-myeloid*: sublining macrophages without B-cell aggregates;
    - *pauci-immune fibroid*: fibroblast-rich, fibrotic, few immune cells.
  - Krenn synovitis-score features: lining thickness, stromal cellularity, inflammatory infiltrate.

**Knee (sagittal)**
- Femur, tibia, patella; articular cartilage, menisci, joint space.
- Synovial membrane and capsule, suprapatellar bursa, Hoffa fat pad.
- Cruciate ligaments, quadriceps and patellar tendons.
- **RA**: pannus eroding cartilage and bone at the margins, effusion, synovial hypertrophy.
- **OA**: focal cartilage loss, osteophytes, subchondral sclerosis and cysts, joint-space narrowing, with less synovitis.

**People with RA**: symmetric small joints are affected (MCP, PIP, wrists, MTP), plus knees. **DIP joints are spared**, which distinguishes RA from OA and psoriatic arthritis.

## Other organs

**Colon mucosa**
- A flat surface epithelium (no villi).
- Densely packed test-tube **crypts** perpendicular to the surface, rich in goblet cells.
- Lamina propria with immune cells between the crypts; muscularis mucosae below.
- **Colitis**: crypt distortion and branching, crypt abscesses, goblet depletion, basal plasmacytosis, a dense infiltrate.

**Lung alveoli**
- Thin septa with capillaries and RBCs; flat type I and cuboidal type II pneumocytes; alveolar macrophages.
- **Pneumonia**: alveoli filled with neutrophils and fibrin.
- **Fibrosis**: thick septa, fibroblastic foci, honeycomb cysts.

**Skin**
- Strata basale, spinosum, granulosum (keratohyalin) and corneum; melanocytes in the basal layer.
- Rete ridges and dermal papillae with capillary loops; papillary and reticular dermis.
- Adnexa; hypodermis fat.
- **Psoriasis**: acanthosis with elongated rete, parakeratosis, a thin granular layer, Munro microabscesses, dilated papillary vessels.

**Artery**
- Intima, internal elastic lamina, media (smooth-muscle cells), external elastic lamina, adventitia with vasa vasorum.
- **Atherosclerosis**: fibrous cap, necrotic lipid core with cholesterol clefts, foam cells, calcification, a narrowed and eccentric lumen.

**Liver lobule**
- Central vein with radiating hepatocyte plates and sinusoids between them; Kupffer cells.
- Portal triads (portal vein, hepatic artery, bile duct) at the corners.
- **Steatosis**: macrovesicular fat displacing the nuclei.

**Trabecular bone**
- Trabeculae with osteocytes in lacunae and an osteoblast lining.
- Osteoclasts in Howship lacunae; marrow (fat plus haematopoietic cells).

## Tumour

- **Normal glands**: polarised columnar cells, basal nuclei, an intact basement membrane. Breast and prostate also have a myoepithelial/basal layer.
- **In situ carcinoma**: atypia (large pleomorphic hyperchromatic nuclei, mitoses), loss of polarity, an intact basement membrane. Cribriform lumens are round and punched out; comedo necrosis is eosinophilic.
- **Invasive carcinoma**:
  - a visibly **breached** basement membrane with cells crossing it;
  - irregular infiltrating glands and nests;
  - desmoplastic stroma with long bundles following the nests, and plump stellate CAFs;
  - an immune infiltrate at the invasive margin, following the tumour edge as an irregular band;
  - irregular vessels and intravasation; necrosis.
- **Tertiary lymphoid structure**: a B-cell follicle with a polarised germinal centre (darker dark zone, light zone with an FDC meshwork, thicker mantle cap over the light zone), a T-cell zone, and HEVs.

## Methods (2025–2026 technology)

**Single-cell genomics and epigenomics**
- **10x Chromium chip**: wells for sample + RT mix, gel beads and partitioning oil. The recovery wells sit next to the oil row, and channels meet at a junction.
- **GEM**: the gel bead is the dominant object, larger than the cell.
- **Barcoded bead oligo**: bead – Read 1 – cell barcode – UMI – poly(dT).
- **Library molecule**: P5 – Read 1 – barcode – UMI – poly(dT) – cDNA – Read 2 – i7 – P7.
- **ATAC-seq**: a Tn5 dimer clamps nucleosome-free DNA. Adapters are inserted at visible cut gaps; nucleosomal DNA is protected.
- **ChIP-seq**: a cross-linked fragment, an antibody against the target protein, and a protein A/G magnetic bead.
- **CUT&RUN**: pA-MNase cuts on both sides of the bound protein.
- **CUT&Tag**: pA-Tn5 tagments next to the marked nucleosome.
- **Illumina**: a patterned flow cell with lanes and nanowells. In bridge amplification the forward strand is anchored at P5 and the reverse at P7, each at one end only.

**Spatial transcriptomics**
- **Visium**: 55 µm barcoded spots on a 100 µm centre-to-centre hexagonal grid, a 6.5 mm capture area and a fiducial frame. CytAssist transfers probes from a standard slide.
- **Visium HD**: contiguous 2 × 2 µm barcoded squares, binned to 8 or 16 µm.
- **Xenium**: padlock probes on mRNA, rolling-circle products appearing as puncta, cyclic fluorescent decoding, and a cell-segmentation output.
- **Stereo-seq**: a patterned DNA-nanoball array (sub-micron pitch). StereoCell registers an ssDNA/H&E image to the bin grid, then nuclei segmentation produces cell bins.

**Spatial and affinity proteomics**
- **PhenoCycler/CODEX**: DNA-barcoded antibodies. In each cycle, complementary fluorescent reporters are added, imaged, then stripped (about 3 per cycle).
- **IMC**: lanthanide-tagged antibodies, laser ablation, CyTOF time-of-flight.
- **Olink PEA**: two antibodies on one protein carry complementary oligos that hybridise only when close, followed by polymerase extension and qPCR or NGS readout.
- **SomaScan**: modified slow-off-rate aptamers (SOMAmers) with biotin, a photocleavable linker and a fluorophore, read by hybridisation to an array.

**Computational pathology**
- An H&E patch has realistic nuclear density and shape.
- WSI → tissue mask → patch grid → foundation-model encoder (a ViT: patch tokens plus a CLS token through transformer blocks) → embeddings → attention-MIL → slide prediction plus heatmap.

## People and anatomy

- Adults are about 7.5 heads tall.
- Skin, hair and clothing are separate parts, so the skin tone can be recoloured.
- Faces are minimal (Servier style).
- **Internal organs**:
  - liver upper right, under the diaphragm;
  - stomach upper left;
  - spleen left, behind the stomach;
  - pancreas behind the stomach;
  - kidneys retroperitoneal at T12–L3, the right slightly lower;
  - the colon frames the small intestine;
  - bladder in the pelvis;
  - heart left of the midline, between the lungs;
  - thyroid below the larynx.
